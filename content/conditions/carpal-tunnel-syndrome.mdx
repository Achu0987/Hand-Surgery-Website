## In plain words

The carpal tunnel is a narrow passage on the palm side of the wrist. The median nerve runs through it, along with the tendons that bend the fingers. When there is less room in the tunnel, the nerve can be squeezed, and that can change how the fingers feel.

## What people often notice

- Numbness, tingling or "pins and needles" in the thumb, index and middle fingers
- Symptoms that wake them at night, or that ease after shaking the hand
- Tingling while holding a phone, a book or a steering wheel
- Over time, a clumsier grip or dropping small objects

The little finger is often not affected, because it is supplied by a different nerve. That detail is one of the things a clinician will ask about.

## What a first appointment may involve

A first visit is usually a conversation and an examination. You will be asked what you have noticed, when it started, and how it affects your day. Some people are asked to have tests, such as imaging or a nerve study, but not everyone needs them.

## Treatment options

Options can range from simple measures you can start at home to procedures, and not everyone needs surgery. The right choice depends on your examination and on what matters to you.
