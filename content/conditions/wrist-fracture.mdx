## In plain words

A fall onto an outstretched hand can break one of the bones of the wrist. That might be the end of the radius, one of the two forearm bones, or one of the small carpal bones in the wrist itself. Some breaks are obvious straight away. Others are subtle, and the wrist simply does not recover the way it should.

## What people often notice

- Pain and swelling after a fall that do not settle
- Tenderness on the thumb side of the wrist
- Difficulty gripping, or turning the wrist and forearm
- A wrist that looks different in shape, which needs urgent attention

## What a first appointment may involve

A first visit is usually a conversation and an examination. You will be asked what you have noticed, when it started, and how it affects your day. Some people are asked to have tests, such as imaging or a nerve study, but not everyone needs them.

## Treatment options

Options can range from simple measures you can start at home to procedures, and not everyone needs surgery. The right choice depends on your examination and on what matters to you.
