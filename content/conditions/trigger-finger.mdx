## In plain words

The tendons that bend your fingers glide through a series of tunnels, a bit like a cord through rings. When the first of these tunnels, at the base of the finger, becomes tight, the tendon can catch as it moves. The finger may click, catch or lock in a bent position.

## What people often notice

- A finger or thumb that clicks or catches when it bends
- A finger that locks bent and has to be straightened with the other hand
- A tender spot or small bump in the palm at the base of the finger
- Stiffness that is often worse in the morning

## What a first appointment may involve

A first visit is usually a conversation and an examination. You will be asked what you have noticed, when it started, and how it affects your day. Some people are asked to have tests, such as imaging or a nerve study, but not everyone needs them.

## Treatment options

Options can range from simple measures you can start at home to procedures, and not everyone needs surgery. The right choice depends on your examination and on what matters to you.
