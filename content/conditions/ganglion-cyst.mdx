## In plain words

A ganglion is a fluid-filled sac that grows from the lining of a joint or tendon. On the wrist it most often appears on the back, though it can also appear on the palm side. Ganglions can change size, and sometimes disappear on their own.

Any new lump should be looked at by a clinician, so they can confirm what it is.

## What people often notice

- A smooth, rounded lump on the wrist
- A lump that gets bigger or smaller over time
- Aching, especially with use, or no pain at all
- Occasionally tingling, if the lump presses on a nearby nerve

## What a first appointment may involve

A first visit is usually a conversation and an examination. You will be asked what you have noticed, when it started, and how it affects your day. Some people are asked to have tests, such as imaging or a nerve study, but not everyone needs them.

## Treatment options

Options can range from simple measures you can start at home to procedures, and not everyone needs surgery. The right choice depends on your examination and on what matters to you.
