## In plain words

Under the skin of the palm is a layer of tough tissue. In Dupuytren's, this tissue thickens and can form lumps and cords. Over time, the cords can slowly pull one or more fingers in toward the palm, so they no longer straighten fully.

## What people often notice

- Firm lumps or pits in the skin of the palm
- A cord running from the palm into a finger
- A finger that will not fully straighten, often the ring or little finger
- Difficulty laying the hand flat, putting on gloves, or shaking hands

It is usually not painful, which is one reason people wait before asking about it.

## What a first appointment may involve

A first visit is usually a conversation and an examination. You will be asked what you have noticed, when it started, and how it affects your day. Some people are asked to have tests, such as imaging or a nerve study, but not everyone needs them.

## Treatment options

Options can range from simple measures you can start at home to procedures, and not everyone needs surgery. The right choice depends on your examination and on what matters to you.
